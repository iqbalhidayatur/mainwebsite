01 / THE BEGINNING

It Started With Curiosity

My interest in technology started when I was in high school. I was curious about how websites and digital products were created. I often wondered how a simple idea could turn into an interactive experience that people could use.

That curiosity eventually led me to explore programming and interface design. When I started studying at Universitas Teknologi Bandung, I had more opportunities to understand how technology works through different courses and practical projects.

One area that caught my attention was Front-End Development. I enjoyed seeing how lines of code could become something visual and interactive.

02 / THE LEARNING PROCESS

Understanding the Fundamentals

I began by learning HTML and CSS. At first, I thought building a website was simply about arranging text, images, and buttons on a page. However, I soon realized that there was much more to understand.

I started exploring how layouts work, how elements interact with each other, and how CSS properties affect the appearance of a page. I learned about positioning, Flexbox, image sizing, spacing, and responsive layouts.

“

Learning to code is also learning how to think, experiment, and solve problems.

The more I learned, the more I understood that developing a website requires attention to both functionality and visual presentation.

03 / THE CHALLENGES

Not Everything Works the First Time

During the learning process, I encountered many problems. Sometimes an image did not fit the section as expected. Sometimes elements overlapped, spacing looked inconsistent, or a layout broke when the screen size changed.

These problems made me realize that understanding CSS is not just about memorizing properties. I needed to understand why an element behaved in a certain way and how different properties affected the overall layout.

I started solving these problems by inspecting my code, testing different approaches, and learning from the results. Every error gave me a better understanding of how the browser renders a page.

04 / APPLYING WHAT I LEARNED

Turning Knowledge Into Projects

As I became more comfortable with the fundamentals, I started applying what I learned through personal projects. I wanted to create something that reflected my interests in both design and development.

One of those projects is Serenity, my personal website. I use it to present my profile, skills, education, and projects while experimenting with different visual layouts and front-end techniques.

Working on Serenity allows me to connect what I learn about interface design with the technical process of building a website. It also gives me a place to evaluate my work and identify areas that I can improve.

05 / REFLECTION

Still Learning, Still Building

Looking back, I have learned that becoming a developer is a continuous process. There are always new things to explore, problems to solve, and better ways to approach a task.

I am still improving my skills in Front-End Development and UI/UX Design. I want to keep building projects, exploring new technologies, and sharing what I learn along the way.

This story is not about reaching the finish line. It is about documenting the process and appreciating every step of learning.

MY PERSONAL PRINCIPLE

Learn. Build. Break. Improve.

The journey continues.
